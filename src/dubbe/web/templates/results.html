Where errors come from (FLEURS, Korean → English, with the earlier translation model)

We replaced one step at a time with a correct human reference:

  • Translation score (chrF): 49.4 with our transcript, 60.1 with a perfect transcript — recognition errors cost about 11 points.
  • The translator itself was the larger gap — the reason it was replaced by the larger NLLB model above.

What does not work well yet

  • Flags miss confident mistakes. On FLEURS, 3 of 15 sentences were flagged and 2 of them were wrong — but about 8 of 15 were wrong by the author's reading (for example "Ghana" for Canaan). A test with independent raters is planned.
  • Short spoken lines are still mistranslated ("That would be great" → 정말 멋져요): sentences are translated one by one, without the conversation around them.
  • Similar voices are taken for one speaker — two men in a Korean TV scene became one. Move the sentences in the editor.
  • Emotion: the emotion model is right 82 % of the time when it tags (acted English), but finds only a third of the emotional sentences, is not measured on Korean, and tagged 2 of 7 calm sentences of a synthetic male voice [angry]. The [excited] / [angry] effects did not change what an emotion model hears in an English dub (5 of 44 sentences with and without), and Chatterbox's emotion strength setting changed nothing measurable in Korean.
  • Recognition is weak for Swahili (26 %), Turkish (17 %) and Hindi (13 %) — fine as target languages, poor as sources.
  • The voice choice failed on an agitated multi-speaker TV scene — the editor lets you change it.
  • Expressive tags: [whisper], [excited], [sad], [angry] and [question] are made by DUBBE for every voice; for [question] English uses the Chatterbox voice, whose end rose in 11 of 12 test questions. A sound classifier heard the English [cough], [gasp], [laugh] and [sigh] only weakly, and the other languages' voices make none of them.
  • No fluent speaker has rated any target language yet; not yet tested on a second computer.
